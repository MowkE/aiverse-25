# 9. Note on ethical interpretation

The language of this paper is behavioral throughout. "Participation" is a recorded action,
"efficacy" is a bounded state variable updated by a stated rule, and "withdrawal" is a flag that
suppresses two behaviors and clears when the state variable recovers. None of these terms is
offered as evidence of experience, preference, wellbeing, or suffering in the simulated agents, and
no measurement in this study could bear on such questions.

We adopt a symmetric stance on the uncertainty. Asserting that artificial agents have morally
relevant interests is not licensed by behavioral data of this kind; asserting that they certainly
do not is equally unlicensed by it, and the second assertion is easier to make carelessly because
it requires no further work. We therefore neither claim nor deny moral status, and we avoid
vocabulary that would smuggle in either position. Should future work in this program make claims
about wellbeing, those claims will need an argument of their own rather than an inheritance from
these results.
