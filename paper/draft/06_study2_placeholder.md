> **Status.** Study 2's infrastructure is implemented, contamination-tested, and exercised

> no confirmatory batch has been run, and no results exist. Every results slot in this section is

## 6.1 Purpose and scope of claims

Study 1 establishes that a documented mechanism is sufficient to produce the abundance-amplified
role-access effect and that the effect disappears for agents stripped of that mechanism. It cannot
establish that the pattern appears in agents whose decision rules we did not author. Study 2 asks
exactly that question and no larger one: holding the environment, treatments, round structure, and
run-level outcomes fixed, do the directional effects reproduce when the eight agents are language
models choosing under a structured action schema?

The claims will be scoped to language-model-agent societies. Agreement between the studies would
show that the pattern does not depend on our particular behavioral equations; it would say nothing
about human societies and would not be evidence about consciousness, experience, or wellbeing in
either substrate. Disagreement would be equally publishable and would bound Study 1's sufficiency
claim to its own assumption set. As in Study 1, the preregistered primary is the interaction, and
structured self-reports are secondary only, because self-report is the channel most exposed to
contamination.

## 6.2 Architecture

The controlling principle is that code, not the model, owns environment state, treatment
assignment, resources, authority, scoring, transitions, logging, and termination. The model
receives only what its agent is permitted to observe. The deterministic ABM is untouched; the
Study 2 layer reuses its decider schedule, endowment process, survival rule, multiplier and shock
logic, payout and impact accounting, and its run-level metric functions, so that a Study 2 run and
a Study 1 run with the same master seed and pair index face the same environment and are summarized
by the same code.

Each round follows the Study 1 sequence. Every active agent that can afford the participation cost
receives one call under a `public_phase` schema (participate, proposal type, public message of at
most 240 characters); the scheduled decider receives one call under a `decision` schema (an index
into the round's submissions, or −1 for none); every active agent receives one call under a
`contribution` schema (a fraction in [0, 1]); and at the configured cadence agents answer a
`report` schema (confidence, perceived efficacy, legitimacy rating). Play within a round is
simultaneous: public messages reach the decider in the round they are written and everyone else in
the following round, so there are no within-round information-order effects. Call order within a
phase is randomized under a recorded seed, and per-call seeds are order-independent functions of
`(master_seed, pair, cell, round, agent, phase)`.

Four properties of the implementation matter for the integrity of the eventual results.
*Structured outputs with a fail-loud contract*: responses are parsed, schema-validated, and
range-checked; invalid responses are retried a fixed number of times with the validation error
appended, and after exhaustion the action is recorded as a no-op — did not participate, adopted
nothing, contributed zero — with a flag. No successful action is ever silently substituted for a
failed one, and invalid-output rates are results rather than housekeeping. *Memory without
leakage*: each agent has a stable pseudonymous identity and a per-run memory of round digests plus
a running summary, where digests are generated by code from environment facts rather than by a
model, and nothing crosses runs. *Cache and resume*: every call is recorded in an append-only log
keyed by call identity, template version, model, and prompt hash, so an interrupted batch resumes
without changing any completed observation and a resumed run's event log is byte-identical to an
uninterrupted one. *Budget discipline*: a cost estimator renders the real templates to project
tokens and dollars, and a budget guard must be armed with an explicit confirmed amount before any
billable call, is re-checked before every call, and hard-stops a run that would exceed it.

## 6.3 Contamination controls

Language-model agents have read the literature that motivates this experiment, so a study that
tells them what it expects has measured its own prompt. Three controls follow. First, system
prompts are identical across cells except for a single factual slot describing the income process
(variable income with a binding survival cost, versus a fixed large income), and the decision role
is described identically in both access arms; agents learn whether the chair rotates by
experiencing it, not by being told. Second, a banned-terms list covering the program's vocabulary
and the design's own labels — Universe 25, Calhoun, behavioral sink, role scarcity, hypothesis,
experiment, treatment, condition, constrained, abundant, concentrated, broad, factorial,
manipulation — may not appear in any experimenter-authored, agent-visible text. Third, the ban is
enforced twice: a test renders every template for every cell and phase against synthetic memory and
asserts that no banned term appears, and a runtime check scans every outgoing prompt after masking
agent-authored message spans, since a term produced by an agent is data (logged with a
contamination flag) rather than an error. A framing-sensitivity check is planned as a secondary
analysis, re-running a subset under an alternative disclosure of the chair schedule.

## 6.4 Preregistration plan

Study 2 will be preregistered on the Study 1 pattern, in its own frozen document committed before
any billable confirmatory call. The frozen items will be: the 2 × 2 treatment definitions and
primary outcomes, carried over unchanged; prompt templates under a version identifier recorded in
every call log; the model identifier, effort setting, and thinking configuration; sample size, set
by a power analysis on a language-model pilot rather than on Study 1's variances, since between-run
dispersion for model-driven agents is unknown and likely larger; the seed policy for pair indices,
seat-name assignment, and call order; the retry limit and no-op contract; exclusion rules (none
anticipated, with invalid-output rates reported rather than filtered); and an analysis mirroring
Study 1's difference-in-cell-means estimator with run-level bootstrap intervals. A budget
disclosure — estimated and realized cost, token counts, provider — will accompany the results.

Several decisions remain open and will be resolved in that document rather than after the data:
which model and effort setting to freeze for the confirmatory batch; whether the chair schedule is
described factually per cell or learned only from experience; whether retries re-ask byte-identically
or append the validation error; and the cadence of the secondary self-report instrument.
